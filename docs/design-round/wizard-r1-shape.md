# Start a game: shape brief (the redo's Round 1, low-fi directions)

## Job and audience

An Operate surface: someone on a couch turns a show (or a team's games) into a game their friends will play, on a phone, often
the night before an episode. Arrivals and their certainty are in `wizard-r1-spec.md` ("Who arrives, and with what"); the first
view's five requirements are in `wizard-r1-first-view.md` (one decision first; the game's name biggest; one way of saying a game;
nothing moved or missing; the first week beside one Start).

## Outcome and proof

Success is a started game the couch understands: what everyone does, by when, how it's won. The proof for this round is the ten
tasks walked through each direction with taps and likely wrong turns counted, and a coverage sketch per direction (the twelve
groups and six hard tracker requests, each with its path). Every game named in the frames is one the engine plays on that page
(`.impeccable/mocks/wizard-r1/data.json`), with its words: Survivor 51 (Episode 9, Wed Nov 18, 8 PM; its 22 made-for games), Below
Deck 13 (Episode 7, Mon Nov 23), The Challenge 42 (all aired: at your own pace), the 76ers (the opener played Oct 22; next home game
Sat Nov 21, 1 PM vs the Bucks).

## Selected directions

Four structures, pinned by the plan and the spec (no concept roll: the brief names them). The visual authority is the committed
Lower Third world (DESIGN.md), drawn low-fi: Night greys, the game on a white caption plate (the world's signature, so each
direction's game reads the same: R3), one yellow (Start it, the One Chyron Rule), dashed outlines for what can't apply or is coming,
cyan rings and numbers marking the taps of the task a frame illustrates (a story mark, not the design).

| | Thesis | Sequence | Focal moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · The menu | ready games to compare, the recommended one open | length as headed tabs with counts → a list whose rows are the plate collapsed → Change it → the parts → Add to it | the open plate with its first week |
| B · The sentence | the game is one sentence; each clause is its own control | the sentence, pre-answered → tap a clause → its choices → back, the clause changed; a clause that no longer fits is marked, never moved | the sentence with its first week |
| C · The interview | ask the couch's questions, not the engine's | tonight's game (one tap) above "who's playing?" → what should everyone do (answers are games) → how long → here's your game, "anything else?" | each question alone, its answers as games |
| D · The slip | build a game the way a bet slip adds legs | ready slips + what everyone does → legs (how long, how it scores, who can share) → more legs from the catalog; the slip docked at the foot is the game | the slip filling as legs are added |

## Scope and boundaries

Low-fi frames at 390 × 844 (first views, plus whole sheets where they scroll); no app code. Breadth: every first view, the frames
the hard tasks pass through, one shared frame for task 10. Untouched: the Lower Third world, the app's tabs, Round 3's archive,
Round 4's settings. Anti-goals: a settings panel first; rails cut at the edge; pictograms without names; toasts as the only record
of a change; a sportsbook's pressure in D.

## States and ranges

Names up to 24 characters ("The Sole Survivor Oracle"); 8 kinds, 22 made-for games (6 tonight, 6 until the merge, 10 the
season); 37 moments on Survivor 51, 21 on Below Deck; a couch of 1 to 30; a question game of up to 12 questions; a season of
unknown length ("to the end"); a show whose results say nobody goes home (Below Deck); an aired season (own pace); a sport's
schedule (an away game next, a home game later). The bot's wait (about 40 s) and its errors appear only on the frames that ask it.

## Interaction and layout

The game plate is one component in all four: name (condensed caps, largest), its one-line hook or engine line, what everyone does
(the effort line, bold), its parts, its first week with real days and times, then Start it below it (yellow, sticky, naming who's
told). A part opens its choices as a screen in the sheet; choosing returns to the game with the change marked ("Added · Undo").
Add to it, + Add a rule, More… and the slip's legs are the same catalog in each direction's own frame, grouped as the spec's
taxonomy, offering only what fits, each with its effect in a line. Targets 44 px; text 15 px or more on the ground.

## Constraints and open decisions

Icons carry their names; bets say "bet" and points are never money; what scores names its scoring; faces carry their qualifier
(tribe, department, position); the show's nouns (castaway, crew member, player; episode, game). Left to the pick: the order (how
long, all at once, who and effort, what), the doors, how depth is reached.
