# Tonight V4: episode night, people and game payoff

This is the current **review proposal**, not an approved production redesign. It supersedes V3's incorrect assumption that a person could select individual games to reveal. Revealing an episode opens its results across **all applicable games**. Personal watched progress remains a separate action.

## One page, one episode context

Intent sets the hierarchy and consequences; Impeccable handles composition, expressive typography, imagery and motion; Perception-First Design checks what attracts attention and whether that attention leads to the right action.

The revised order follows the user's clarification:

1. Show, season and artwork retain the entertainment identity.
2. A small prominent strip reports due decisions, readiness or locked decisions. It opens a separate quick-action queue rather than displaying every editor on Tonight.
3. **One progress control** groups last watched, next to watch and behind/caught-up/release status. Last and next are no longer separate buttons opening the same chooser.
4. Watch-along and watched actions explicitly name their episode. Live, caught-up viewers get one watch action without a redundant watched button.
5. Friends' faces, actual episode positions and relative distance appear directly on the page. **See everyone** opens a fuller pace map; a person opens their detail.
6. Last-watched recap, recognizable cast, GIF state and discussion precede game updates.
7. Game minis provide the episode's payoff and direct access to fuller results, people, contributions and the existing game pages.

Tonight is treated as the living detail of the person's latest episode. It does not replace Games or the richer individual game pages. The first phone view includes the decision entry, progress, watch controls, friends and the last-episode GIF action at the checked sizes. Longer discussion and game comparisons scroll naturally.

## Two useful game-summary options

**Compact** emphasizes the outcome, a small cast/lineup strip and permitted friends' results. **Detailed** adds lineup contribution portraits and animated comparison bars, or a survival-pick portrait with captured streak/history. These are intentionally different density options for review, not a required casual/expert user mode.

Adapters cover the recorded survival pool, lineup, drafted team, predictions and bingo families. The live fixture contains six games across those five families. Generic families use captured compiled episode totals where available. Missing question-level answers, bingo squares and contribution reasons remain unavailable; the mock does not invent them. This is not verification of every generated-game combination.

Quick actions include picks, lineups and a captured single-cast prediction. More complex question shapes keep a direct full-game destination. Saving is local to the preview; it does not recompute recorded scores. Own-pace games remain playable by default in the proposal, while explicit live-only locks stay visible. Production eligibility must come from the authoritative game contract.

## Reveal as a shared episode event

The reveal confirmation names the episode and all affected games. There is no per-game selection. **Reveal & play recap** opens an actual full-screen presentation; **Just show results** opens a summary with the same reveal consequence.

The presentation opens with show artwork and **Last time on Survivor**, then a visual episode recap, individual game chapters, permitted player comparisons and shared standings, followed by GIF reactions. Watching-only participants get the recap and reactions without empty game chapters. Sports has a **Last time out** wording fallback; this iteration's recorded visual review is Survivor, not a completed sports review.

People, cast and scores open details directly. Back returns to the same chapter or editor draft. Skipping to results and replaying change presentation only. Count-ups and comparison bars animate captured values on entry; reduced motion shows final values without those animations. Chapters are manually paced.

## Data and verification boundary

The mock reuses cached show art, cast photography, generated island artwork and GIF bytes. Social examples are labeled illustrative. Missing episode titles are not invented. Fresh reveal data is bounded to recorded episode 3, fetched only after explicit confirmation. Other fresh reveals remain explicitly unavailable. Opponent results respect the fixture's game-sharing boundary; watched position alone does not grant access.

Preview writes stay in browser storage; production mutations are blocked by the Atlas proxy. Local reveal state is not written into the underlying application's game pages.

`node scripts/dev-test/check-tonight-v4.js` passed with nine viewport/case combinations, lifecycle cases, decision saves, detail returns, global reveal, full-screen bounds, motion/reduced-motion behavior and preserved prior versions. No browser errors or production writes were recorded. `node --test tests/ui-atlas.test.js` passed all nine tests. Evidence is saved under ignored `.ui-atlas/tonight-pilot-v4/`, including `checks.json` and screenshots. These checks establish bounded behavior, not user approval or a usability-study result.
